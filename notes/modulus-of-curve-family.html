1. Metrics, lengths and the modulus

Throughout, $D$ and $D'$ are domains in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $n\geq 2$, $f:D\to D'$ is a diffeomorphism, and $\Gamma$ is a family of smooth paths in $D$. The image family is

$$f(\Gamma):=\{f\circ\gamma:\gamma\in\Gamma\},$$

which is again a family of smooth paths, now in $D'$.

Example 1.1 (the typical family)Given disjoint non-empty compact sets $E,F\subset D$, let $\Gamma$ be the family of all smooth paths in $D$ joining $E$ to $F$. This is a “large” family, and the one to keep in mind.

Length and volume with respect to a density

Let $\rho:D\to[0,\infty)$ be continuous.

Definition 1.2 ($\rho$-length and $\rho$-volume)For a smooth path $\gamma$ in $D$ and a Lebesgue measurable set $A\subseteq D$, define
$$\ell_\rho(\gamma)=\int_\gamma \rho(x)\,|dx|,\qquad \upsilon_\rho(A)=\int_A\rho^n(x)\,dx,$$
where $dx$ denotes Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Admissible densities and the modulus

Definition 1.3 (admissible density)A continuous $\rho:D\to[0,\infty)$ is admissible for $\Gamma$ if
$$\ell_\rho(\gamma)\geq 1\qquad\text{for every }\gamma\in\Gamma.$$
The set of all admissible densities is denoted $\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$.

Intuition. Admissibility keeps $\rho$ from being too small, on average, along any curve of $\Gamma$. If $\Gamma$ contains enough curves that together sweep out a substantial part of $D$ (in the sense of measure), an admissible $\rho$ cannot be small on most of $D$, so $\upsilon_\rho(D)$ cannot be small either.

The natural question is: how small can $\upsilon_\rho(D)$ be made by a clever choice of $\rho$?

Definition 1.4 (Modulus of a curve family)
$$M(\Gamma)=\inf\{\upsilon_\rho(D):\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)\}.$$
Remark$M(\Gamma)=\infty$ in two situations: if $\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)=\varnothing$ (convention for the empty infimum), or if $\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)\neq\varnothing$ but $\upsilon_\rho(D)=\infty$ for every $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$.

2. How the modulus changes under a diffeomorphism

We want to compare $M(\Gamma)$ and $M(f(\Gamma))$. The plan is to transport admissible densities from $D'$ back to $D$, and measure the volume cost with the distortion of $f$.

Outer and inner distortion

For a linear map $T$, recall $H_O(T)=\|T\|^n/|\det T|$. The outer distortion of $f$ is

$$K_O(f)=\sup_{x\in D}H_O(f'(x))=\sup_{x\in D}\frac{\|f'(x)\|^n}{|J_f(x)|},$$

where $\|\cdot\|$ is the operator norm. The inner distortion $K_I(f)$ is defined analogously and satisfies $K_I(f)=K_O(f^{-1})$. We assume $K_O(f)<\infty$.

Pulling back an admissible density

Let $\widetilde\Gamma=f(\Gamma)$ and let $\widetilde\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\widetilde\Gamma)$. Define $\rho:D\to[0,\infty)$ by

$$\rho(x)=\widetilde\rho(f(x))\,\|f'(x)\|.$$
Lemma 2.1$\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$.
Proof. Let $\gamma:[a,b]\to D$ be in $\Gamma$ and put $\beta=f\circ\gamma\in\widetilde\Gamma$. Since $\beta'(t)=f'(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)$ and $|f'(\gamma(t))\gamma'(t)|\leq\|f'(\gamma(t))\|\,|\gamma'(t)|$, we obtain
$$\begin{aligned}1\leq\ell_{\widetilde\rho}(\beta)&=\int_a^b\widetilde\rho(\beta(t))|\beta'(t)|\,dt\\&\leq\int_a^b\widetilde\rho(f(\gamma(t)))\|f'(\gamma(t))\|\,|\gamma'(t)|\,dt\\&=\ell_\rho(\gamma).\end{aligned}$$
Thus $1\leq\ell_\rho(\gamma)$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$. □

Comparing volumes

Lemma 2.2With $\rho$ and $\widetilde\rho$ as above, $\upsilon_\rho(D)\leq K_O(f)\,\upsilon_{\widetilde\rho}(D')$.
Proof. Use $\|f'\|^n\leq K_O(f)|J_f|$ and then the change of variables $y=f(x)$:
$$\begin{aligned}\upsilon_\rho(D)&=\int_D\widetilde\rho(f(x))^n\|f'(x)\|^n\,dx\\&\leq K_O(f)\int_D\widetilde\rho(f(x))^n|J_f(x)|\,dx\\&=K_O(f)\int_{D'}\widetilde\rho(y)^n\,dy.\end{aligned}$$
□

Quasi-invariance and invariance

Theorem 2.3 (quasi-invariance of the modulus)Let $f:D\to D'$ be a diffeomorphism with $K_O(f),K_I(f)<\infty$ and $\Gamma$ a family of smooth paths in $D$. Then
$$\frac{1}{K_O(f)}M(\Gamma)\leq M(f(\Gamma))\leq K_I(f)M(\Gamma).$$
Proof. By Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, for every $\widetilde\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\widetilde\Gamma)$,
$$M(\Gamma)\leq\upsilon_\rho(D)\leq K_O(f)\upsilon_{\widetilde\rho}(D').$$
Taking the infimum over $\widetilde\rho$ gives $M(\Gamma)\leq K_O(f)M(\widetilde\Gamma)$. Applying the same argument to $f^{-1}:D'\to D$ and the family $\widetilde\Gamma$, and using $K_O(f^{-1})=K_I(f)$, gives $M(\widetilde\Gamma)\leq K_I(f)M(\Gamma)$. Together these are the claim. □
Corollary 2.4 (conformal invariance)If $f:D\to D'$ is a conformal diffeomorphism, then $K_O(f)=K_I(f)=1$, hence
$$M(f(\Gamma))=M(\Gamma)$$
for every family $\Gamma$ of smooth paths in $D$. In words: the modulus is a conformal invariant.

3. Extremal length and quasiconformal maps

Because of Corollary 2.4, $M(\Gamma)$ is called the conformal modulus of $\Gamma$. A closely related quantity is the extremal length

$$\lambda(\Gamma)=M(\Gamma)^{1/(1-n)},\qquad\text{so for }n=2:\quad\lambda(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{M(\Gamma)}.$$

Theorem 2.3 is exactly what makes the following class natural. A diffeomorphism $f$ with $K_O(f)$ and $K_I(f)$ finite is quasiconformal; equivalently, its linear dilatation $H_f$ is bounded on $D$. Its maximal dilatation is

$$K(f)=\max\{K_O(f),K_I(f)\},\qquad H(f)^{n/2}\leq K(f)\leq H(f)^{n-1}.$$

By Theorem 2.3, a quasiconformal map changes moduli by at most a factor $K(f)$ in either direction.

4. Two model computations

Both examples follow the same two-step pattern, which is the standard way to compute an exact modulus:

  1. Upper bound: exhibit one admissible $\rho$ and compute $\upsilon_\rho(D)$.
  2. Lower bound: restrict to a subfamily $\Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma$ (so $M(\Gamma_0)\leq M(\Gamma)$, since $\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)\subseteq\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma_0)$), and show by Cauchy–Schwarz that every admissible $\rho$ has $\upsilon_\rho(D)$ at least the same value.

4.1 Curves crossing a rectangle

Let $R=[0,a]\times[0,b]$ and let $\Gamma$ be the family of paths in $R$ joining the sides $x=0$ and $x=a$. Then

$$M(\Gamma)=\frac{b}{a}.$$
Proof.Upper bound. Take $\rho\equiv1/a$. A path $\gamma\in\Gamma$ travels from $x=0$ to $x=a$, so its Euclidean length is at least $a$ and $\ell_\rho(\gamma)\geq a/a=1$. Thus $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$ and
$$\upsilon_\rho(R)=\int_0^b\int_0^a\frac{1}{a^2}\,dx\,dy=\frac{b}{a},\qquad M(\Gamma)\leq\frac{b}{a}.$$
Lower bound. Let $\Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma$ be the horizontal segments $x\mapsto(x,y_0)$, $y_0\in[0,b]$, and let $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$. Then for each $y$,
$$1\leq\int_0^a\rho(x,y)\,dx.$$
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$1\leq\left(\int_0^a\rho(x,y)\,dx\right)^2\leq a\int_0^a\rho^2(x,y)\,dx,$$
so $\int_0^a\rho^2(x,y)\,dx\geq1/a$. Integrating in $y\in[0,b]$ gives $\upsilon_\rho(R)\geq b/a$ for every $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$, so $M(\Gamma)\geq b/a$. Therefore $M(\Gamma)=b/a$. □

4.2 Curves crossing an annulus

Let $A=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:r\leq|z|\leq R\}$ and let $\Gamma$ be the family of paths in $A$ joining the circles $|z|=r$ and $|z|=R$. Then

$$M(\Gamma)=\frac{2\pi}{\log(R/r)}.$$
Proof.Upper bound. Take
$$\rho(z)=\frac{1}{|z|\log(R/r)}.$$
For any path $\gamma\in\Gamma$, $|dz|\geq d|z|$ along the path, so
$$\int_\gamma\rho\,|dz|\geq\frac{1}{\log(R/r)}\int_r^R\frac{dt}{t}=1,$$
and $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$. (For a radial path $z=te^{i\theta}$ we have $|dz|=dt$ and equality holds.) In polar coordinates $dA=s\,ds\,d\theta$:
$$\begin{aligned}\upsilon_\rho(A)&=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_r^R\frac{1}{s^2\log^2(R/r)}s\,ds\,d\theta\\&=\frac{2\pi}{\log^2(R/r)}\int_r^R\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{2\pi}{\log(R/r)}.\end{aligned}$$
Thus $M(\Gamma)\leq2\pi/\log(R/r)$.
Lower bound. Let $\Gamma_0$ be the radial segments $\gamma_\theta(t)=te^{i\theta}$, $r\leq t\leq R$, and let $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$. Writing $\rho=(\rho\sqrt{s})s^{-1/2}$ and applying Cauchy–Schwarz along $\gamma_\theta$,
$$\begin{aligned}1&\leq\int_r^R\rho(se^{i\theta})\,ds\\&\leq\left(\int_r^R\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_r^R\rho^2(se^{i\theta})s\,ds\right)^{1/2}. \end{aligned}$$
Hence $\int_r^R\rho^2(se^{i\theta})s\,ds\geq1/\log(R/r)$ for every $\theta$. Integrating over $\theta\in[0,2\pi]$,
$$\upsilon_\rho(A)=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_r^R\rho^2(se^{i\theta})s\,ds\,d\theta\geq\frac{2\pi}{\log(R/r)}$$
for every $\rho\in\operatorname{Adm}(\Gamma)$. Therefore $M(\Gamma)\geq2\pi/\log(R/r)$, and hence
$$\boxed{M(\Gamma)=\frac{2\pi}{\log(R/r)}}.$$
□

4.3 Note: logarithmic coordinates

Put $z=e^w$ with $w=u+iv$. Then $z=e^u e^{iv}$, and

$$r\leq|z|\leq R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\log r\leq u\leq\log R,\qquad 0\leq v<2\pi.$$

So, in logarithmic coordinates, the annulus is a rectangle of width $a=\log(R/r)$ and height $b=2\pi$, and the radial segments $\gamma_\theta$ become the horizontal segments of Example 4.1. The formula $M=b/a$ then reads $2\pi/\log(R/r)$, exactly as in Example 4.2.
