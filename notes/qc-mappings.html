Higher-Dimensional Quasiconformal Mappings

Seminar Project · Under Prof. S. Ponnusamy, IIT Madras

These notes follow a guided reading seminar extending classical conformal and quasiconformal theory from the complex plane to $\mathbb{R}^n$ for $n \geq 3$. Early sections collect worked examples of isometries of $\mathbb{R}^n$ — translations, rotations, reflections, and inversions — laid out as groundwork for the distortion estimates that follow.

Dilatation

The pointwise linear dilatation of a map $f$ at $x_0$ compares the stretching in the most- and least-expanded directions:

$$ K(x_0, f) \;=\; \frac{\max_{|h|=1} \, |Df(x_0)h|}{\min_{|h|=1} \, |Df(x_0)h|} $$

A map is $K$-quasiconformal if this ratio stays bounded by $K$ at every point — the higher-dimensional generalization of the classical complex-analytic dilatation condition.
